Why we built it

Your photos are safe. The stories behind them are not.

Every cloud album on earth solved storage. None of them solved the part that actually disappears — the voice that could tell you who is on the left, and why everybody was laughing.

Storage

A backup is not a memory

Ten thousand photos, perfectly preserved, and not one of them says what was happening. You kept the file and lost the story.

Silence

The person who knew stops being asked

The knowledge lives with the eldest, the founder, the one who organised it. Nobody sits them down — until the day it's too late to.

Scroll

Group chats bury what they collect

You post the photo, six people reply beautifully, and in a fortnight it is 900 messages up. The context existed. It just didn't stay anywhere.

How it works

Three steps, and only one of them is yours.

The hard part of collecting memories was never the collecting — it was getting other people to take part. So we removed the part they refuse to do.

Inside the app

Built around the person who won't install anything.

Voice, photo and text weigh the same

Three ways to answer, presented as equals — because the best account of a night is usually spoken, not typed. Voice notes play back in place, with the speaker attributed.

Circles, not one big feed

Family, friends, work and community stay separate. A photo goes to the circle it belongs to, and the people in that circle are the only ones who ever see it.

FamilyFriendsTeamCommunity

Disagreement is a feature, not a bug

Every contributor marks their own certainty — certain, I think, not sure — and the app keeps it. When two people remember a night differently, the story holds both, attributed, and refuses to pick a winner. That is the single design decision the whole product turns on.

Ask a question, not just post a picture

“Who remembers that old car?” goes out to the circle and comes back as a memory from each of them — one question, many answers, all kept together.

Two shelves, and the split matters

Memories are the photos you started. Shared with me are the ones you spoke into. The counter on your dashboard leads with voices collected, not photos stored — this is not a storage product.

The guarantee

An AI that cannot invent your past.

Every product in this category asks you to trust a prompt. Prompts are guidance. These are boundaries — triggers, check constraints and row-level security in Postgres, where a bad model response simply cannot get through.

21 trust claims, each enforced in the database
and verified by integration test

Nobody's words are ever rewritten

Yours or a contributor's. The owner may exclude a remark or delete it — never edit it. guard_remark_update

No invented people, dates or places

Proper nouns in the narrative are diffed against what people actually wrote. A violation falls back to a composer that can only rearrange, never add.

Faces are never identified from the image

Any observation that asserts who someone is gets dropped, not softened. The app will tell you the image wasn't analysed before it will guess.

Certainty stays the contributor's own claim

If the model returns a confidence level, it is discarded and theirs restored. Only the person who remembers gets to say how well they remember.

Nothing is public without your approval

A check constraint, not a code path — and withdrawing approval forces the story private again. public_requires_approval

A leaked invite link is not an open door

Guest endpoints are rate-limited and resolve the token server-side. Contributors never learn the token, and never see who else was invited.

Install it

Three minutes, and one warning screen we should explain.

OurTales is not on the Play Store yet, so you install it directly. That is normal for a beta, and it means the file comes from us rather than from a store listing — so here is exactly what you are getting and how to check it.

  1. Download the file

    Tap the button on this page from the phone you want it on. Your browser will ask you to confirm the download — that is the browser, not the app.

  2. Open it, and allow this one source

    Android will say it “can't install unknown apps from this source”. It is asking about the source, not the file. Tap Settings, turn on the permission for your browser, then go back and tap Install.

    What that screen actually means

    Android shows it for every app that did not come from the Play Store, including ones from companies you have heard of. It is a statement about where the file came from — which is why we publish the checksum below, so you can prove the file you got is the file we built.

  3. Open OurTales and start with one real photo

    Pick a username, add the photo, say what you remember, then send the invite link to the people who were there. They need nothing installed.

Before you ask

The questions people actually have

Do the people I invite have to install anything?

No. You send a link; it opens in whatever browser they already have. No download, no sign-up, no password. This is the single most important thing about the product — the person who remembers the most is usually the least likely to install an app.

Can the AI make things up about my family?

It cannot add anything. The composer arranges prose that real people wrote, and the safeguards sit in the database rather than in a prompt: invented names are diffed out, invented witnesses are dropped, identification of faces is discarded, and the contributor's own certainty is restored over anything the model claims.

If no AI composer is configured at all, a deterministic composer runs instead — it can only rearrange, so invention is impossible by construction.

Who can see my photos?

Only the circle you posted them to, and only the people you sent the link to. Nothing becomes public unless you approve it, and that rule is a database constraint rather than a settings toggle — withdrawing approval unpublishes the story immediately.

What does it cost?

The beta is free, and everything you collect during the beta stays free to you permanently. We haven't set pricing beyond that, and we'd rather tell you honestly than invent a number for a landing page.

Is it safe to install? Android is warning me.

That warning appears for every app that did not come from the Play Store, including ones from companies you have heard of. It is a statement about where the file came from, not about what is inside it.

So we make the source checkable: the build is published on GitHub Releases, it is signed with APK Signature Scheme v2/v3, and the SHA-256 checksum is printed on this page. If the checksum of your download matches, you have the exact file we built — bit for bit.

Why is it 141 MB?

Because this build carries native code for all four Android architectures in one file, so a single link works on any phone rather than making you pick. A per-architecture build is a fraction of the size and is what will go to the Play Store — this one is optimised for “send the link and it just works”.

iPhone or Android?

Android for now — the iOS build isn't ready, and there is a one-field waitlist on this page for the day it is.

But the people you invite need neither. Contributing happens in a browser, so an iPhone, an old tablet or a desktop all work today.

How do I get updates?

For this build, by downloading the next one from this page and installing over the top — your account and everything in it stays put. Automatic updates arrive with the Play Store listing.

Can I get my memories out?

Yes. Ask and we export everything in your account, including the contributions other people made to your photos. It's your family's archive; holding it hostage would defeat the point of building it.

What's still rough?

It's a beta and we'd rather you hear it from us: there is no password reset yet (we don't ask for an email at sign-up, so there's nowhere to send one), invite links can't be revoked once sent, and the story won't describe what is in the photo because image analysis is switched off — it says so rather than guessing. The beta exists to break exactly these.
